What your league actually rewards

Three settings dominate everything else: there are no points for receptions, you start only two receivers, and there are ten teams. All three point the same direction, and most published rankings you'll see online are built for 12-team PPR — which is a different game.

Sixteen of the first thirty players off the board are running backs. In a ten-team league that's your first three rounds. You start two backs plus a flex that will almost always be a third back, you start only two receivers, and a catch is worth nothing by itself. Draft accordingly.

1. Tight end got cheap — this is your biggest edge

In PPR, Trey McBride and Brock Bowers go at picks 33 and 39. In your format they go at 52.7 and 52.2 — the fifth-sixth turn of a ten-team draft. Same players, twenty picks later, purely because receptions are worth zero.

One caveat worth knowing: ESPN's panel tagged McBride, LaPorta, Kraft, Pitts and Fannin as bust candidates, so the position carries real risk. But the risk is priced very differently here. At 33 you're paying a premium for it; at 52 you aren't. Bowers or McBride at the 5/6 turn is the best structural value on your board.

2. Quarterback is still free, and 4-point passing TDs make it freer

Josh Allen goes at 20.6 — early third round in a ten-teamer. After him: Maye 48, Lamar 51, Burrow 54, Prescott 63, Daniels 70, Hurts 73, Stafford 74, Purdy 82, Lawrence 91, Mahomes 98, Caleb Williams 98, Herbert 105, Goff 108. That's thirteen starters between picks 48 and 108.

Your scoring gives 4 points per passing touchdown and 1 point per 25 yards with no bonuses, which compresses the position further — the elite quarterbacks can't separate the way they do in 6-point-passing-TD leagues. Let it come to you around rounds 7–9.

3. Running back, then running back again

The usable starting tier ends around pick 57. Through Bucky Irving (47), Judkins (49), Tuten (51), TreVeyon Henderson (57) and David Montgomery (57) you're getting real workloads. Immediately after that it's Pollard, Stevenson, Jaylen Warren, Dowdle, Hubbard — committee backs to a man. In a format where your flex is a third running back, leaving pick 57 with two is how seasons go wrong.

4. Receivers wait — and standard scoring changes which ones you want

Two starters means you need far fewer, and no-PPR reshuffles who's even good. The possession slot receiver loses most of his value; the deep threat gains. Wan'Dale Robinson goes 79.5 in PPR and 103 here. Alec Pierce goes 58 in PPR and 46 here, on the back of finishing second in the NFL at 21.3 yards per reception. Draft the touchdown and the big play, not the target hog.

Your settings, and what to do about them

  • Two IR slots — use them. An injured high-upside player drafted in the last round costs you nothing, because he moves to IR and frees the bench spot. This is free option value most of your league will ignore.
  • Only 4 of 10 teams make the playoffs. A 40% field is harsh, and seeding breaks on total points. Draft for ceiling; a safe roster that finishes fifth wins nothing.
  • 30 acquisitions all season, and waivers never reset. Claiming moves you to last permanently, so your early-season priority is a one-shot asset. Don't spend it in week 2 on a backup you're hoping about.
  • Roster maximums: 8 RB, 8 WR, 4 QB, 3 TE, 3 K, 3 D/ST. Only the tight end cap is likely to matter.
  • The draft is offline, so nothing syncs anywhere. Turn off auto-draft on the Draft tab and use it as a live tracker — the search box records any player to whoever is on the clock.
  • Kickers: 0–39 and 40–49 yard field goals both score 3. No distance bonus in that range, so volume on a good offense beats a big leg. Last round regardless.
  • Return yards score 0.02 per yard — fifty return yards is one point. A tiebreaker between two players you already like, nothing more.
  • No keepers in 2026 or 2027, so there is zero reason to value youth. Draft the best 2026 season available, full stop.

The quarterback league

Standard scoring, 12 teams, 16 rounds, and real money on the line. Two settings make this unlike anything else you're playing, and both are easy to miss.

Passing touchdowns are worth 6, not 4 — and the yardage bonuses are enormous. A 300-yard, 3-touchdown game pays 30 points on yardage-and-scores alone before the bonus ladder, which adds another 5 at 300+, 6 at 350+, 7 at 400+. The generic non-PPR ADP on this board prices quarterbacks for 4-point leagues. It is wrong here, and it's wrong in your favour if you act first.

1. Quarterbacks are badly underpriced on this board

The board shows Allen at 21, Maye 48, Lamar 51, Burrow 54 — those are 4-point-passing-TD prices. With 6-point passing TDs plus escalating yardage bonuses, the spread between QB1 and QB12 in your scoring is far wider than the ADP gap suggests. Take a top quarterback earlier than the board says, and take your second before the run starts — remember you are required to roster two.

2. You must roster two of every position

The commissioner's rule is 2 QB, 2 RB, 2 WR, 2 TE, 2 K, 2 D/ST — twelve mandatory players, plus four of any combination, for a sixteen-man roster in a sixteen-round draft. That means four of your sixteen picks are kickers and defenses, and two more are tight ends you may not want. Only four picks are genuinely free.

Practical consequence: your real skill-position draft is about ten picks long. Don't arrive at round 14 still needing two kickers, two defenses and a second tight end — that's four wasted picks under pressure. Plan the last four rounds before you start. This tool's My Team tab tracks the full requirement, not just the starting nine.

3. Big-play backs and receivers, again

Receptions score zero, and there are distance bonuses: +3 for a 40–49 yard touchdown, +6 for 50 or more, plus 3 points at 100 rushing or receiving yards. This rewards the explosive player over the volume possession guy even more strongly than your ESPN league.

Also worth knowing

  • Top 8 of 12 make the playoffs, weeks 15–17, and seeds 4–8 are set by total points regardless of division. Points matter even when you're winning.
  • No IR stashing. The rules explicitly forbid drafting an injured player to IR — the opposite of your ESPN league. Draft healthy.
  • $10 per add/drop, $20 per trade player. Churn is expensive; draft depth you actually intend to keep.
  • Draft is 9/5/26 at 6:15pm ET, snake, no pick clock.

The shallow-roster PPR league

12 teams, full PPR, and a twelve-man roster — eight starters and only four bench spots. That last number drives everything.

With four bench slots you cannot hoard upside. Handcuffs, lottery tickets and injured stashes all cost you a quarter of your bench. Draft players who start for you, and let the waiver wire be your bench.

1. Tight end is optional — treat it that way

Your starting requirement is 1 QB, 2–3 RB, 2–3 WR+TE, 1 K, 1 D/ST. Tight end shares a slot with receivers rather than having its own. In full PPR, a mid-tier tight end rarely beats a third receiver in that slot, so unless Bowers or McBride falls to you, skip the position and start three receivers. This tool's lineup for this league has no dedicated TE slot for exactly that reason.

2. Distance-scaled touchdowns nudge you toward explosiveness

Touchdowns pay 6 to 9 points by length rather than a flat 6, and interceptions and fumbles each cost only 1. That's a mild tilt toward big-play players and away from punishing turnover-prone quarterbacks. Mild — don't overhaul a board for it, but break ties with it.

3. Full PPR restores the receivers your other leagues bury

This is the one league where the possession receiver is genuinely valuable again. Wan'Dale Robinson, Ladd McConkey, Josh Downs and Michael Pittman are worth what the PPR board says, not what your standard leagues say. Keep the two boards separate in your head.

4. Twelve rounds only

144 total picks. The draft ends while good players are still on the board, which means the last two rounds are a kicker and a defense and you should feel no regret about it.

Superflex changes the whole game

12 teams, full PPR, 14 rounds, and a superflex slot — you may start a second quarterback in it, and you almost always should. There are no kickers and no defenses in this league at all.

Josh Allen goes 1.5 overall here. Nine of the first twenty-five picks are quarterbacks. Twelve teams wanting two starting quarterbacks means twenty-four quarterbacks are starters, and there are nowhere near twenty-four good ones. That scarcity is the entire format.

1. Two quarterbacks, early, non-negotiable

The board tells the story: Allen 1.5, Maye 6.0, Lamar 6.8, Burrow 7.6, Prescott 11.8, Daniels 12.7, Hurts 16.1, Purdy 17.3, Stafford 17.4, Lawrence 17.4, Mahomes 20.2, Caleb 22.7, Herbert 24.1, Goff 24.2, Dart 26.7, Nix 30.9. That's sixteen inside 31 picks. After Mayfield at 38 the position thins fast, and the teams that waited spend the rest of the draft starting a running back in their superflex and losing four points a week for it.

Target two of the top sixteen. Taking quarterbacks with two of your first four picks is not a reach in this format — it's the market price.

2. What that costs you, and where you make it back

Spending early on quarterbacks means missing the elite running backs. That's fine: with three receiver slots plus a flex in full PPR, receiver depth is where you rebuild. Nacua at 5.4 and Chase at 8.9 are the two skill players genuinely worth an early pick here; after that, take quarterbacks and let the running back room come from the 40–100 range.

3. Tight end is nearly free

Bowers falls to 62.4 and McBride to 66.4 — later than in any of your other leagues, because every early pick is being spent on quarterbacks. One starting slot, no premium, and the two best options available in round six. Take one and stop thinking about it.

4. No kickers, no defenses

Fourteen rounds of nothing but real players, and this tool removes kickers and defenses from the board entirely for this league. Your last few picks are bench receivers and backfield speculation — spend them on players with a path to starting, not on handcuffs.

Playoff schedules

Full-season strength of schedule barely separates teams — the spread across the league is a few points. The weeks your league plays for money are a different story, and that is where a schedule edge is actually worth drafting for.

Draft calendar

Opponent quality is measured by each defense's projected fantasy output — a proxy for how hard that defense is to play against. It cannot tell you a defense is tough on running backs but soft on receivers; it is a tiebreaker between similar players, not a reason to move someone up a round.

Positional cliffs to memorize

Rules for draft day

  • Never reach past a tier break. If four players share a tier, the fourth is nearly as good as the first and he'll survive to your next pick. Take the scarce position now and come back for him.
  • Kicker and defense in the last two rounds. No exceptions. Streaming a defense off waivers beats any defense you draft early.
  • Runs are real, panic is not. The Draft tab flags a run when one position takes four of the last twelve picks. A run is usually a reason to take the other position — unless it's emptying a tier you needed, which is what the cliff alert is for.
  • Check byes only at the end. Two starters sharing a bye is a one-week problem. Passing on a better player in round 5 to avoid it is a season-long one.

What this tool does not do

There are no projected point totals here, and so no value-over-replacement column. I don't have a projection set I trust for your scoring, and inventing one would make the board look more precise than it is. Tier scarcity does that job instead — a weaker signal, honestly labelled.
